Plan the light and the work that makes it possible

A lighting project may involve fixture replacement, new wiring points or a different control layout. The contractor needs to assess existing wiring, mounting support, equipment suitability and access. Fixture appearance alone does not establish compatibility with a dimmer or the suitability of a location. Share product information and the intended room use before installation is priced.

Prepare the scope for your Goderich property

Sketch the counter and cabinet layout, marking where you prepare food and where the cabinet fronts, switches and any cable entry points sit. Collect the product sheets for the strips or fixtures you like.

Ask the contractor how the existing wiring and switch positions would be used, whether a dimmer suits the product chosen and how long the counter would be unavailable for cooking.

What should the assessment establish?

A Goderich kitchen is getting task lighting and strips beneath its cabinets. Where the new lights would be fed from is identified, and so is whether the existing wiring can be reused or must be extended. Whether the chosen strips or fixtures suit the proposed control, any dimmer included, is confirmed. The contractor explains how cable would reach the cabinet underside and what finishing follows. The written scope gives the kitchen circuits to be switched off and when, so meals and routines can be planned around them, and names who repairs any opened wall or cabinet surface.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can counter lighting go in without cutting into finished surfaces?

Sometimes the cable can run through existing spaces, but that depends on what the contractor finds. Ask for the proposed cable route to be explained and for any wall repair to be included in the scope.

Will my chosen light strips behave properly on a dimmer?

Compatibility depends on the product, not on how it looks. Give the contractor the product sheets and ask them to confirm in writing that the control suits the strips or fixtures before anything is installed.
