A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Goderich property

From the outside, photograph any labels and the panel door, then list each room and the main devices in it, including any office, studio or hobby room. Leave every cover closed.

Ask the contractor how circuits would be identified and labelled during the work, and which rooms would lose supply while that is done. Ask for the labelling to be part of the written scope.

What should the assessment establish?

Every circuit in an older panel appears on a list matched to what it serves, showing where one circuit feeds more than one room and whether an office, studio or hobby room hangs on one circuit or several. Any condition concerns get a section of their own, in which the contractor puts them in words a homeowner can follow. For each stage of the job, the scope gives the circuits to be switched off and the rooms they supply. The last section names who prepares the final circuit directory for the Goderich house and how that directory stays with the property records.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can the circuits in an older, unlabelled panel be identified before work?

A contractor can trace what each circuit feeds as part of an assessed scope, which is different from guessing from old handwriting. Ask what the tracing will involve and which rooms will lose supply while it is done.

Do I need to label the circuits myself first?

Guesses written on a panel can mislead the next person who trusts them. Record what you already know about each room and leave the labelling to the contractor, who can confirm it during the assessed work.
