EV charger installation in Goderich.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Goderich property

Sketch the parking spot, the wall or post position and the likely route to the panel, noting doors, windows and any room you will still be using. Photograph the exterior surface and the intended spot.

Ask the contractor how the cable would reach the charger, what weather exposure the equipment needs to suit and which doors or rooms would be affected on the days the route is opened up.

What should the assessment establish?

Listing the charger and a price, with nothing on the cable route, mounting surface or weather rating, a quote cannot show which doors or working rooms an outdoor installation in Goderich would affect. Instead, a scope traces the route, identifies the surface and gives the rating needed. It states whether the present supply can take the extra load or a load-management arrangement is proposed, where the new circuit begins and which supply must be off during connection. The scope names someone for repairing opened surfaces and keeping doors and working rooms reachable. The ESA notification is listed as part of the job.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does a charger on a driveway post need a different route?

A post may mean a longer cable route than a wall mount, and that route may be buried or run on a surface. Have the provider describe what it passes and how it would be protected before you agree.

Is it acceptable to feed a charger from the circuit in my workroom?

The charger should be assessed with the other loads on the property, not added to a circuit because it happens to be nearby. Ask the contractor whether the charger needs its own dedicated circuit and what that means for the workroom circuit.
