Before work begins

Settle which spaces stay usable while work proceeds.

A home with a dedicated office, studio or hobby room may have much of that room's equipment on one circuit, and a desk, bench or easel can fix where its lights and receptacles go. The same may hold in other homes.

Write down which rooms you need to keep working in each day, and which equipment cannot be switched off for long. Ask the contractor to say when each room's supply would be switched off.

Can I keep working from a home office during electrical work?

The supply to some rooms may be switched off for part of the work, so ask the contractor which circuits are affected and when. Move or save anything that cannot lose power unexpectedly, such as a computer.

Should work be done room by room or all at once?

Room by room can keep more of the home usable, but it may mean more visits and repeated access. Ask the contractor to compare both approaches in writing and to explain what each means for the rooms you use daily.

Why do two quotes for the same rooms list work in a different sequence?

Each provider may sequence the work to suit their own approach. Put the quotes side by side against your list of rooms and equipment, then ask why the order, access needs and switch-off periods differ.

What records help if the work happens over several visits?

Keep a dated list of what was changed in each room, with photographs of labels and finished positions. Ask the contractor to confirm the final scope in writing after each stage.

Does the contact form commit anyone to a call-out or a contract?

No, the form commits nobody to a call-out or contract. The site is for reading: it receives and reviews enquiries but arranges no visits, prices nothing and does none of the electrical work.
